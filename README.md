# Betat

> *"Betat" — from the Bamileke language of the Bantu people, Cameroon.*
> *It means "the other person" — or humanhood.*
>
> *In English, TAT also means Turn Around Time — the time between a question and its answer.*
>
> *We are building this for the other person: someone we will never meet, in a future we cannot fully imagine, who deserves to know what human beings actually knew, created, and experienced — without waiting.*

---

## Terminology

Two roles exist in Betat. They are distinct and must never be confused:

**Provenancier** *(pro-VEN-an-see-ay)* — a verified human being who records, archives, or submits human-originated content to a Betat community. The subject of every provenance record. The person the HI tag belongs to. A scientist submitting an observation, an elder recording oral knowledge, an artist archiving a creative work — all Provenanciers.

**Contributor** — a developer or technologist who builds, improves, or extends the Betat open-source codebase. Participates via GitHub issues and pull requests. A Contributor never appears in a provenance record.

These two roles may be held by the same person — a developer who is also a Provenancier — but the roles themselves remain separate. A Contributor's pull request and a Provenancier's provenance record are different kinds of human contribution, governed by different processes, and must always be described with their own names.

## The Problem

A child born today will grow up in a world where it is increasingly impossible to tell whether something was created, observed, or experienced by a human being — or generated by a machine.

This is not a distant concern. It is already happening.

Every year, more of the internet's baseline content is synthetic. Search results surface AI-generated articles. Social feeds carry AI-generated images, voices, and opinions. Encyclopedias are edited by automated systems. Archives are filled without human hands.

The machines are not malicious. But they are fast, scalable, and indifferent to provenance.

Human beings are slow, individual, and irreplaceable — and that is precisely what makes what they produce worth preserving.

An antique is not valuable because it looks better than a modern reproduction. It is valuable because of what it provably is: made by a specific human being, at a specific moment in time, under specific conditions. That chain of origin — its provenance — is the thing no reproduction can carry.

Betat exists to build that chain for human knowledge, before the window closes.

## What Betat Is

Betat is an open-source framework for preserving, tagging, and indexing human-originated content — organized by community, anchored by provenance, and accessible to anyone who wants to find what human beings actually knew, believed, created, and experienced.

It is not a social network.
It is not a fact-checking service.
It is not a replacement for the open web.
It is not a political project.
It is not a hosting platform.

It is a **trust layer** — built by humans, for humans, so that future generations have a reliable way to find what was real.

## The Core Principle

> *Human-originated content, verified by community, tagged as HI, preserved with provenance, and accessible without barriers.*

Every architectural decision in this project either serves that principle or it does not belong here.

## Foundational Guidelines

These guidelines govern Betat from its first day. They are a living document — Contributors will refine, extend, and improve them as the project grows. But the core commitments they encode are not negotiable:

**1. Reading is always free and public.**
No registration is required to browse, search, or read content indexed by Betat. The human record belongs to humanity. Friction-free access is not a feature — it is the project's fundamental contract with the future.

**2. Contributing content requires verified authentication.**
Any Provenancier who adds content to Betat — individual, institution, community body, or organization — must pass a verified standard agreed upon by the relevant community. The standard differs by content type and is set by the human community responsible for that domain. Reading is open; writing is earned.

**3. All verified human-originated content is tagged HI.**
Every accepted provenance record carries an explicit Human Intelligence (HI) tag — machine-readable in the provenance record and visibly displayed in any interface built on Betat. The HI tag is the project's core deliverable: a clear, universal, trustworthy signal that a human being made this.

**4. The index tags — it does not host.**
Betat does not copy or store the world's existing human content. Instead, Betat's UI layer and any tools built on it apply HI or unverified tags to content as it is rendered — drawing on the provenance index to distinguish what has been verified as human-originated from what has not. This approach is realistic, zero-storage, and immediately buildable. Contributors are invited to improve and extend this strategy.

**5. The archive is append-only.**
Provenance records are never deleted or modified. Corrections are new records that reference the original. The full history of human knowledge — including its errors and its revisions — is part of the record. Future generations of Provenanciers contribute forward; they do not rewrite what came before. This norm is both a technical design and a community commitment.

**6. Trust is verified by human communities, organized by content type.**
There is no central authority that decides what counts as human-originated content. Trust is assessed and maintained by self-governing human communities, each responsible for a specific content type: science, art, religion, indigenous knowledge, personal testimony, craft, law, history, and others. The community is the trust mechanism within its domain.

**7. These guidelines will evolve.**
This is a seed. Contributors, Provenancier communities, and future maintainers will improve every aspect of this project — including these guidelines. What will not change is the foundational commitment: human-originated content, verified, tagged HI, and permanently accessible to the other person.

## How It Works

Betat is built on three layers, each independently implementable:

### Layer 1 — Communities
Self-organized groups of human beings, each governing a domain of knowledge or experience. Each community authenticates its Provenanciers, sets its own contribution standard, verifies human origin within its domain, and writes provenance records for accepted contributions.

### Layer 2 — Provenance Store
An append-only, open-format record of every verified HI contribution. Independently hostable. Content-addressed. Permanent.

### Layer 3 — Index and Tag Layer
An open index layer across all provenance stores, queryable without registration. Because every community publishes its records via open protocols, anyone can crawl them and build an index — betat main runs a reference index as a public convenience: *an* index, never *the* index. The UI layer built on this index renders content with visible HI or unverified tags — tagging what it displays based on provenance records, without hosting the content itself. The strategy for tagging existing web content at scale is an open problem, presented honestly and offered to Contributors to improve.

---

## What Betat Becomes

Betat is built in stages, each on top of the previous:

**A website first** — a public face where anyone searches the index, sees HI badges on verified content, and reads about the project. No registration. Immediately useful.

**A browser extension second** — the tag-on-render model made real. A Provenancier or any user browses any page; the extension queries the Betat index; HI badges appear on verified human content inline. This is the feature that makes Betat felt in daily life.

**A search engine eventually** — HI-first search across the full index, ranked by provenance strength and community verification rather than engagement or SEO. This is the long-term vision, reachable only after the index has depth.

## The HI Tag

The HI tag — Human Intelligence — is Betat's most visible contribution to the world.

It is two letters. It means: *a verified human being made this.*

It appears in every provenance record as a machine-readable field. It appears in every interface built on Betat as a visible badge. It is the signal that a child in 2060 uses to find what was real.

The absence of HI is not an accusation — it means provenance is unverified, not necessarily that the content is AI-generated. The goal is to make human content findable, not to condemn what has not yet been tagged.

## Why Now

The window is open. We are living at the last moment when there is still a clear, recognizable line between what humans made and what machines generated. Every year that passes without this infrastructure narrows that window.

Future generations cannot build this archive retroactively. Provenance must be recorded at the time of creation. The chain of origin cannot be reconstructed after the fact — only preserved from the present forward.

This is the urgency. It is real. It belongs to every ordinary person who has ever wanted their words, their knowledge, or their experience to mean something beyond their own lifetime.

- **Community organizers** who want to seed the first Provenancier communities
- **Identity and verification specialists** who can improve the Provenancier authentication model
- **Search and indexing engineers** who can build and scale the open index layer
- **Translators** who can carry this README into every language
- **Anyone with a better strategy** for indexing existing human content at scale — the problem is named, the floor is open
- **Anyone** who believes the record of human experience is worth preserving

No contribution is too small. A corrected sentence in this README is a human act in a project about human acts — and that is not lost on us.
